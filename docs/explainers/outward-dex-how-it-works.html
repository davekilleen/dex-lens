Outward Dex — how the whole thing works

A house call for someone's personal AI system: look, don't touch, then help only where invited — and never require them to move to Dex.

M1 built & verified — CI green M2 building now all decisions resolved except gate sign-off
In one breath

What it is: one command opens a private page on the person's own computer. Dex looks at the AI setup they already have, works out what jobs it's doing for them, and shows honestly what's working, what isn't, and what current Dex could add.

What it never does: never requires switching to Dex, never uploads their stuff, never changes anything without an exact preview and a fresh yes — and every change can be undone.

Why it matters: it's how we look after people we can't identify — forks, ancient installs, home-grown systems — and a respectful front door for people who never installed Dex at all.

Where it fits

The "nobody gets left behind" ladder

Born from the Michelle conversation: when someone shows up on an unknown Dex version, help depends on how much we can figure out.

Tier 1

We know their version. Normal tracked upgrade. Easy.

Tier 2

We can guess it. Doctor fingerprints their files against every release ever shipped, they confirm the guess, we walk them to latest — snapshot first, rewind always possible.

Tier 3

We genuinely can't tell — or they never had Dex. Their system is its own thing now. Change the offer: "want us to look at what you've built and bring the good bits of Dex to you, piece by piece?" That offer is Outward Dex.

How it works

Four acts, kept strictly separate

Finishing one act never quietly starts the next — each needs its own yes.

ACT I

Diagnose

Read-only look, only in folders they approved. Nothing changes, nothing leaves the machine.

ACT II

Decide

A map of their system organised around their jobs — what's solid, shaky, missing. They choose what to do.

ACT III

Adapt

One change at a time: exact preview, receipt, undo. No proven undo → we refuse to automate.

ACT IV

Contribute

Optional: share a great recipe back to Dex — sanitized, previewed, one card at a time. Never on by default.

The experience

The nine-stage journey

Trusted doorway

One command, private page opens. Nothing scanned yet.

Permission to look

Plain-language screen: exactly which folders, what stays local, what we won't touch. No reading before yes.

Private look

Only what was agreed, from a frozen snapshot. Secrets redacted on sight, never stored.

"Your system does these jobs — right?"

Dex proposes; the person corrects, adds, removes. Only confirmed jobs go forward.

Diagnosis

Per job: is the capability working, how do we know, is it within safe bounds? Three honest answers, never one mushy score.

The map

Findings nested under their jobs, reasoning inspectable, one useful next move each.

Receipt & check

Did it actually help? Receipt saved locally, undo available. Anything ambiguous stops the line.

Stop, look at another job, or share a Capability Card back — individually previewed and approved.

Safety

The six gates

An independent security critique hardened the plan; its gates are wired in as tests. The "change things" half cannot ship until every gate passes — and the M1 adversarial check already caught and fixed seven real defects, including two obscure file-write routes that slipped past both the sandbox and the write-detector.

G1

The inspector is caged

The file-reading part can't run commands, write, or reach the network — enforced by the OS and attacked by our own hostile test files.

G2

Every byte accounted for

A ledger of all data we touch. Anything not in the ledger can't be saved or sent — by construction.

G3

Changes from a short menu only

Automated changes come from a fixed reversible list. Messages, money, deletions, credentials: impossible to construct.

G4

Sharing is airtight

Cards structurally can't contain secrets, personal files, or private paths. Withdrawal actually withdraws.

G5

No moving the goalposts

Pilot success is defined and locked before any results are seen.

G6

High-stakes jobs stay manual

Money, messages, credentials, health, legal: diagnosed, never auto-changed. Unsure → treated as high-stakes.

Where your decisions landed
